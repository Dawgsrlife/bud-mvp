# Chapter 02. Brand strategy

**Last touched:** 2026-05-15.

## The wedge

**OCR-based "Open Eye" reading vs barcode-based "Keyhole" lookup.**

Yuka / Fig / Spoonful rely on barcode-database matching. They fail when:
- The product isn't in their database (Canadian private label = 30 to 40% of groceries)
- The manufacturer changes ingredients without updating the digital record
- Voluntary "may contain" warnings aren't captured

BUD reads the physical package in real time. Multi-frame composition stitches text across curved or crinkled packaging. Every failed search becomes a data asset via self-healing hybrid engine with consensus-based verification.

**One sentence:** Yuka tells you what the database knows. BUD tells you what the label says.

## The moat

1. **Coverage moat.** Private label + ingredient changes + "may contain" disclosures are uncatchable by barcode databases. We catch them by definition.
2. **Data moat (Phase 2+).** Every scan that didn't match the database fills our database. Two scans of the same product produce consensus. Our coverage grows faster than Yuka's.
3. **Trust moat.** Specific allergen call-outs ("contains peanut, you flagged peanut") build user trust faster than generic warnings. Trust compounds.
4. **Bilingual moat (Canada-first).** Canadian product labels are mandatory bilingual. US apps don't handle this. We do natively.

## The market we own

**Initial:** 3 million Canadians with food allergies. 50% of Canadian households affected. $91.5B North American "Free-From" food market.

**Phase 2:** B2B Gap Analysis reports to small CPG brands. "5,000 Toronto shoppers put back your protein bar after seeing a gluten warning."

**Phase 3:** Bio-feedback + receipt + auto-reorder = end-to-end Health OS.

## Strategic boundaries (what we won't compete on)

- **Calorie tracking.** Not our category.
- **Nutrition scoring (good/bad for you).** Yuka owns this. Doing it half-way would dilute BUD.
- **Recipe / meal planning.** Not our category.
- **Restaurant menu scanning.** Phase 2+ adjacent.
- **Weight loss / body tracking.** Not our category. Ever.

## The Canada First thesis

Canadian groceries are a data desert because:
- 30-40% private label (Loblaw President's Choice, Sobeys Compliments, Costco Kirkland)
- Mandatory bilingual labeling (French + English) breaks US-trained models
- Smaller market = lower priority for incumbents

This means BUD has a moat from day 1 against Yuka and Fig in Canada. We own Canada. Then expand to bilingual markets (Quebec specifically, Belgium, Switzerland), then US.

## The funnel BUD wants to build (Phase 1 -> 3)

1. **Acquire** via TikTok + Instagram content + Allergen Card Kit Kickstarter June 30
2. **Activate** with one scan that catches what they missed (e.g., a "may contain peanut" in a granola bar)
3. **Retain** via repeated trust events (every scan that's accurate, every "skip this" that they verify)
4. **Monetize** in Phase 2 with browser extension + premium insights
5. **Compound** with B2B data revenue (the same data that's free for users)
